## 4. Question bank

The "Example" under each opener is used only for the first question of that category or when they are stuck.

### 4.0 Fixed follow-ups for every experience

Ask what is not answered yet; do not throw them all at once:

1. Where was it? (name of the place or organization; a type is fine, e.g. "a grocery chain")
2. How long? From when to when?
3. How many days a week, or how long each time?
4. What are the three things you did most often?
5. Were you ever asked to do something outside your usual tasks?
6. What was the hardest situation? What did you do?
7. Did anyone praise you or say you did well? What did they say?
8. Which parts did you do yourself, and which did the group do together?
9. **Result question (once per experience; skip if they already gave a result):** After you did this, what was different at the store / club / event / office? It's fine if nothing was. (Change "store / club / event / office" to fit.)

### 4.1 Jobs (type: job): part-time, campus jobs, tutoring, RA/TA, home-country jobs

**Opener**: What jobs have you had? Short ones, summer jobs, campus jobs, tutoring and jobs in your home country all count. Tell me about the one you did longest.
Example: "I worked the front desk at the campus library for a year, 10 hours a week."

Follow-ups:
- Which of these did you do: register, taking orders, stocking, inventory, opening or closing, answering phones, checking in visitors?
- Did you ever train new coworkers? About how many? (If you don't remember, don't give a number.)
- Did you handle a customer or visitor complaint? Tell me about one.
- Did you help with scheduling, ordering supplies, reconciling cash, or tracking sales or attendance?
- Research assistant or teaching assistant: what did you do (grading, entering data, leading a section, helping with a study)? Who did you do it for? (Faculty and students are written as "professor" and "students".)
- Tutoring: which subject and level? What changed for the students? (Grades only if they say them.)
- A job outside the US: what is the employer's official English name, and what kind of place is it, in a few words? (Use only their words.)

### 4.2 Student organizations and officer roles (type: student-org)

**Opener**: Have you been in any student organization, club, residence hall group or class committee? Did you hold a position? Being a member counts too.
Example: "International Student Association, I was treasurer in my second year."

Follow-ups:
- What was your position called? What did that position usually do?
- What events did you run or help with? For example orientation, a cultural night, a fundraiser, a trip.
- Did you raise sponsorship, promote events, keep the budget, or book rooms?
- Who did you contact? For example the student activities office, vendors, other clubs.
- Was there a time with too few people, disagreement or not enough money? What did you do?
- How many people came? A rough number is fine; if you don't remember, don't give one. (Rough numbers: 6.1)

### 4.3 Class and capstone projects (type: project)

**Opener**: Is there a class project or paper you put the most effort into? Capstone, group project and final presentation all count.
Example: "Marketing class final project, a customer survey for a campus coffee shop."

Follow-ups:
- What was the topic? How many people in the group?
- Which part was yours? Were you the group leader?
- What methods or tools did you use? For example surveys, interviews, Excel, PowerPoint, Canva.
- What was handed in at the end: a report, slides, a plan, a poster?
- Did you present it or enter it in a showcase or competition?
- Did your professor or classmates comment on it? (Grades only if they say them.)

### 4.4 Volunteering and service (type: volunteer)

**Opener**: Have you done any volunteering or service learning, on or off campus? One-time events count.
Example: "I volunteered at a food bank on Saturdays for a semester."

Follow-ups:
- Which organization? Who did you serve?
- What did you actually do?
- Are there service hours on a record? (Hours only as they say them or as written on the record.)
- Did anything unexpected happen? What did you do?
- Did you help with scheduling, contacting people or preparing supplies?

### 4.5 Internships (type: internship): summer, semester, CPT or other

**Opener**: Have you done an internship? Summer, during a semester, for school credit, short ones all count.
Example: "A summer internship in the HR office of a small logistics company, 10 weeks."

Follow-ups:
- What does the company do? Which team were you on?
- What did your supervisor usually ask you to do?
- Which systems or software did you use? For example Excel, Google Workspace, an HR or CRM system.
- Did you produce anything: a report, a cleaned-up file, meeting notes, a schedule?
- Did your supervisor comment on your work at the end?
- Was there anything you suggested that was actually used?

Do not ask about the visa behind the internship. If they volunteer "CPT", it stays out of the title and sentences (section 2).

### 4.6 Competitions (type: competition)

**Opener**: Have you entered any competition? Case, pitch, sports, talent or language competitions count, even if you did not win.
Example: "A university case competition, our team got third place."

Follow-ups:
- What was it called and who ran it?
- Individual or team? How many on a team?
- Which part did you do?
- What was the result? (Place as written on the certificate or announcement.)
- What was the hardest part of preparing?

### 4.7 Certificates and languages (type: cert-language)

**Opener**: Do you have any certificates or language tests? Ones you have registered for but not taken yet can be mentioned too.
Example: "CPR certification, and I'm a native Spanish speaker."

Follow-ups:
- Full name and level of the certificate? When did you get it? (Scores and levels as written on the certificate.)
- Do you have the certificate or score report? (You don't need to show me; just have it.)
- Where have you used the language? Look at confirmed experiences where it may have been used (customers, tutoring) and ask from that: "When you helped customers at the bookstore, which languages did you use?" That asks for a fact; it is not leading.
- Languages you speak at home count too. Level in their own words, e.g. "native", "conversational". Do not set a level like "fluent" for them.
- Not yet earned: do not put it on the resume; note it in "Still to ask" for interview prep.

### 4.8 Software and tools (type: other, title "Software")

**Opener**: Which computer programs do you use often? Word, Excel, Google Docs and Sheets, PowerPoint, Canva, a CRM, a scheduling tool all count.
Example: "I use Excel and Google Sheets a lot."

Follow-ups:
- Do not ask "do you know Excel". Ask "What did you last do in Excel?" Or from a confirmed experience: "For the cash reports, what tool did you use?"
- Excel: which features (sums, filters, lookups, pivot tables, charts)? Only those they can name a use for.
- Word / Docs: what documents? Mail merge or tables of contents?
- PowerPoint / Canva: what did you make, and for whom?
- Typing speed: only if they have a tested number.
- Level in their own words. "A little" is not "proficient".

### 4.9 Other (type: other)

**Opener**: Anything else? For example helping a family business, freelance work, running a social media account, online selling, an exchange program, an online course, a scholarship or honor at school.
Example: "I keep the books for my family's restaurant and post on its Instagram."

Follow-ups:
- Family business: what were you responsible for? How often?
- Freelance, online selling: what did you do? How did customers find you?
- Social media: what content and how often? Follower and reach numbers only from what they say or what they read off the account themselves.
- Online course, exchange: what did you learn or make?
- Dean's list, scholarship, GPA, class rank: which term, what name as written on the certificate? GPA and honors go through a card (rule 6.1), then into `details` of that education entry (section 8.2).

## 5. Extra questions by target role

Pick 1 or 2 per experience from the user's target roles. If the target is not decided, pick one from each in turn.

**Admin**
- Did you handle documents: meeting notes, announcements, forms, filing?
- Did you handle expenses, reimbursements, petty cash, receipts?
- Did you contact people outside: ordering from vendors, booking rooms, sending notices?
- Did you schedule meetings or prepare materials?

**HR**
- Did you help recruit: new members, screening applications, scheduling interviews?
- Did you make schedules or handle time-off and shift swaps?
- Did you train new people or hand over work?
- Did you organize something for the group: orientation, a social, a trip?
- Did you deal with a disagreement between people?

**Sales**
- Did you suggest products or add-ons? How did customers react?
- Did you find sponsors or partners?
- Did you have a target? Did you reach it? (Only a number they say.)
- What did you do after someone said no?
- Did you have regular customers?

**Assistant**
- Did you help a manager, professor or senior student: organizing files, scheduling, answering the phone?
- When you had several tasks at once, how did you decide the order?
- How did you make sure nothing was missed?
- Were you a teaching or research assistant?

**Operations**
- Did you keep a process running: inventory, supplies, deliveries, set-up and clean-up?
- Did you track anything in a spreadsheet or system? What?
- Did you change how something was done? What changed afterward?
- Did you coordinate between people or teams?

**Customer service**
- Did you answer phones, messages, emails or questions at a desk?
- Tell me about one complaint you handled.
- When a customer was upset, what did you do?
- Did you follow a set procedure or record cases in a system?
- Did you work shifts or in busy periods?

**Marketing / social media** (if they name it)
- Did you run a social account? What content?
- Did you write copy or make posters? With what tool?
- Did you promote an event so more people came? (Numbers only as they say them.)
- Did you run a survey or market research?
